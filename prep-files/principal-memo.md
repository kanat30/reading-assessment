# FluencyScope

*A reading fluency assessment tool for grades 6–8.*

---

## What it is

A web app that does the oral reading fluency assessment a teacher would normally do 1:1 with a student — but without the teacher having to be there.

A student opens a link on their Chromebook. They type their name. They read a one-minute passage aloud. The app records them, scores their reading against the Hasbrouck-Tindal grade-level norms and the Rasinski Multidimensional Fluency Scale, and produces a report the teacher can read in 90 seconds.

The teacher gets back: words-correct-per-minute with grade-band percentile, accuracy percentage, a four-dimension prosody score, a tappable audio waveform with error markers at the exact moments where the student stumbled, the full transcript with errors highlighted inline, and a brief teacher-facing summary describing the patterns in plain language. Every score can be overridden in one click if the teacher disagrees.

It does this and nothing else. There is no tutoring, no comprehension instruction, no curriculum, no parent portal, no full MTSS workflow. Those belong to bigger tools that already exist. This is a scalpel, not a platform.

---

## The problem it solves

NYC Reads expands the structured-literacy mandate to middle school in Fall 2025, with universal coverage in Fall 2027. Grades 6–8 ELA teachers are now required to identify and progress-monitor struggling readers — but **no NYC-approved screener currently measures oral reading fluency for grades 6–8**.

The assessment that exists requires a teacher to sit 1:1 with each student for 3–5 minutes, three times a year, plus weekly probes for any student receiving Tier 2 or 3 support. For a class of thirty, that's two hours of one-on-one time per benchmark cycle that the teacher has to find somewhere — usually during instruction.

FluencyScope removes the teacher from the assessment loop without removing the assessment.

## Compliance and rollout timeline

| Milestone | Status | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Working V1 prototype | ✓ Complete | Now |
| Pilot at first school (1 ELA teacher, 1 class) | Pending principal approval | 2–3 weeks |
| ERMA submission | In preparation | Begins at pilot start |
| ERMA approval window | NYC DOE timeline | ~3 months |
| NYC DOE-wide deployment ready | After ERMA approval | ~Q4 2026 |

The pilot can begin before ERMA approval if conducted as a research-and-feedback engagement under existing principal authority. ERMA submission runs in parallel.

**Compliance posture:**
- FERPA + NYS Education Law 2-d compliant DPA available on request
- All student audio encrypted at rest, 90-day default retention, deletable on request
- No student PII or audio used to train AI models — ever
- Dialectal variation (AAVE, Spanglish, Caribbean Englishes, newcomer accents) treated as non-error by design

## What we'd ask of a pilot school

Minimal. The pilot is structured to be near-zero-cost on the school's side:

1. One ELA teacher willing to use the tool with one class, three times during the pilot semester
2. A 30-minute kickoff call to walk through the dashboard
3. A 30-minute exit interview at semester's end

In exchange:
- Free use of the tool through the pilot, with priority support
- A written case study (anonymized if preferred) we can share with other schools
- A direct line to product changes — feedback gets implemented in days, not quarters
- Initiation of the ERMA process so the school can continue using the tool after the pilot
